# DRAFT: Technology Infrastructure & Strategic Partnership Agreement

**Status:** Working draft for legal review before signature.

This Agreement is intended to be entered into between **Obit Technologies Limited** ("Technology Company") and **Obit Technologies Multipurpose Cooperative Society Limited** ("Cooperative").

## 1. Purpose
The parties collaborate so that the Cooperative can use technology infrastructure developed and operated by the Technology Company for member services, digital commerce and related approved programmes, while preserving the Cooperative's separate legal identity, member governance and statutory responsibilities.

## 2. Technology infrastructure
The Technology Company will develop, host, maintain and support agreed technology services, including Obit Market, Obit SafePay orchestration, member-facing integrations, APIs, dashboards, automation and other agreed digital systems.

## 3. Intellectual property
Subject to third-party rights and any separately commissioned work expressly agreed in writing, the Technology Company retains ownership of its platform software, source code, architecture, APIs, product names, technical documentation and technology intellectual property. The Cooperative receives a limited, non-transferable right to use the agreed infrastructure for approved Cooperative purposes during the term.

## 4. Cooperative independence
The Cooperative retains responsibility for its membership, elections/governance, statutory records, member resolutions, cooperative programmes and obligations imposed by applicable cooperative law. Nothing in this Agreement transfers those governance functions to the Technology Company.

## 5. Obit Market
Obit Market is technology infrastructure of the Technology Company. The Cooperative may serve as a community partner and may onboard eligible members to use the marketplace subject to marketplace rules, verification, prohibited-goods controls and applicable law. The Technology Company may offer the infrastructure to other approved communities or organizations, subject to confidentiality and data-protection obligations.

## 6. Obit SafePay
Obit SafePay is a protected-transaction orchestration layer and does not create an Obit-held stored-value wallet under the initial model. Payment, escrow/custody and settlement must be provided by an approved third-party financial provider under the provider's applicable permissions and contract. Neither party may represent itself as a bank, licensed payment custodian or escrow holder unless separately authorized to do so.

## 7. Provider contracting
The legal entity named in each third-party provider contract is responsible for that contract. Corporate documents, tax identifiers, licences or approvals of one party must not be represented as belonging to the other. Where the Technology Company contracts with a provider for platform infrastructure used by Cooperative members, the parties will document the permitted data flows, service responsibilities and commercial allocation.

## 8. Personal data
Personal data is not owned merely because a party owns the technology. The parties will identify their controller, joint-controller or processor roles by processing purpose and execute appropriate data-processing/data-sharing terms before production. Those terms will address lawful basis, transparency, minimization, security, retention, data-subject rights, processors/sub-processors, cross-border transfers, incident response and deletion/return on exit.

## 9. Security and audit
The Technology Company will maintain reasonable technical and organizational safeguards for the services it operates, including access control, audit logging, secrets management, backup/recovery and incident handling appropriate to the service risk. The Cooperative will protect its administrator credentials and promptly report suspected misuse.

## 10. Commercial terms and related-party governance
Fees, revenue shares, reimbursable costs and service charges must be set out in a written schedule approved through each party's proper governance process. Because the parties are related within the Obit Group ecosystem, material related-party transactions should be documented, priced transparently, recorded in the accounts of the correct entity and approved without overriding duties owed to Cooperative members.

## 11. Service levels
A production schedule will define service availability targets, support channels, severity levels, incident response, planned maintenance, backup objectives and recovery expectations. Pilot services carry no production service-level commitment unless expressly stated.

## 12. Marketplace complaints and disputes
The parties will maintain a documented process distinguishing: (a) marketplace complaints, (b) Cooperative membership complaints, (c) payment-provider disputes and (d) technical incidents. Final authority over custody, release or refund of funds remains subject to the contracted financial provider's terms and applicable law.

## 13. Records and reporting
Each party will maintain records necessary for its own legal, tax, audit and governance obligations. Shared operational reports must not be treated as a substitute for either party's statutory records.

## 14. Confidentiality
Each party will protect the other's non-public business, technical, member and commercial information and use it only for the agreed purposes, subject to lawful disclosure requirements.

## 15. Business continuity and exit
The parties will maintain a transition plan addressing continuity, export/return of Cooperative records, revocation of credentials, outstanding transactions, member communications, data retention/deletion and migration to a replacement service if the relationship ends.

## 16. Term, suspension and termination
The signed version will state the commencement date, term, renewal, suspension rights, termination rights, cure periods and consequences of termination. Suspension for security, fraud, legal or provider risk may be immediate where reasonably necessary.

## 17. Liability and indemnities
Liability caps, exclusions and indemnities must be negotiated and reviewed by Nigerian counsel before signature, with separate treatment for fraud, confidentiality, data protection, intellectual-property infringement and regulatory breaches where appropriate.

## 18. Governing law and dispute resolution
The signed version will specify Nigerian governing law, notice addresses and a dispute-resolution process suitable for the parties and their registration status.

## 19. No regulatory shortcut
This Agreement does not confer a financial-services licence, payment licence, banking licence or regulatory approval on either party. Each regulated activity must remain within the permissions of the entity actually performing it.

## 20. Execution
The final Agreement must be approved and executed by duly authorized representatives of both entities after legal review.
